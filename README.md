### Projects

**NHANESanalyzer: An R Package for Health Survey Data**

Co-developed an R package that lets users with minimal coding experience quickly summarize and visualize NHANES health survey data. Designed a suite of functions covering numeric/categorical summarization, missing data reporting, group comparisons, and distribution plotting, all built around a human-readable variable-naming layer so users never need to touch raw NHANES column codes. Collaborated with a team of three to design the function API, write documentation, and build a full package vignette.

**Modeling Sleep Quality and Sleep Disorder Risk from Lifestyle Data**

Used linear and logistic regression to identify which lifestyle and demographic factors most strongly predict sleep quality and sleep disorder risk across 374 adults. Found that stress is the dominant driver of day-to-day sleep quality (each unit increase costing ~0.5 points, p < 0.001), while BMI category is the dominant driver of clinical sleep disorder risk (46x higher odds for overweight/obese individuals). Worked through a complete-separation issue in the logistic model by strategically collapsing BMI categories, and validated both models with full diagnostic testing (VIF, Shapiro-Wilk, Hosmer-Lemeshow), achieving 94% variance explained and 90.6% classification accuracy respectively.

**Do Critically Acclaimed Movies Actually Make Money?**

Built an end-to-end pipeline combining MovieLens user ratings/tags with TMDb financial data (budget, revenue, production company) to explore whether audience sentiment predicts box office success. Designed a normalized relational schema in MySQL, enriched data via the TMDb API under real rate-limit constraints, and ran SQL-driven analyses in Python/Matplotlib. Found a clear "blockbuster effect" in profit concentration and only mixed alignment between user ratings and revenue — financial success often diverges sharply from critical reception.
